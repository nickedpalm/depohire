### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- California: Court Reporters Board of California regulates licensure; exam includes 3 parts, with Dictation/Transcription (Machine/Skill) passable via Certified Verbatim Reporter (CVR), Certified Verbatim Reporter-Stenotype (CVR-S) from NVRA, or Registered Professional Reporter (RPR) from NCRA.[1][7]
- Georgia: All court reporters licensed May 1, 2024–April 30, 2025, must attend mandatory Learning Essentials About Professionalism (LEAP) seminar by February 23, 2026, or face automatic license suspension.[5]
- Nevada: Certified Court Reporters Board oversees certification; existing certificates remain valid, with ~requirements for English proficiency, medical/legal terminology, and obligations to parties.[6]
- National certifications recognized in states: NCRA (RPR), NVRA (CVR/CVR-S), AAERT for electronic reporters.[5]

### 2. Current Pricing/Rates
- No specific 2024-2026 pricing data in results; Nevada requires filing fees for certificates (amount unspecified in NRS 656.150), Georgia implies certification costs via NCRA/NVRA/AAERT approvals without quoted rates.[4][5][6]

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- **Licensure Requirements**: California—pass 3-part exam or hold NCRA RPR, NVRA CVR/CVR-S; full-time official reporters for remote pilots must be licensed under Business & Professions Code §8024.[1][7] Nevada—certificate mandates English understanding (reading/spelling/vocabulary/medical/legal terms), ethical obligations; individual seals, surety bonds, continuing education, code of ethics required (RSA 310-A:170-181).[4][6] Georgia—NCRA/NVRA/AAERT certification, plus LEAP training for new licensees; enforce code of ethics/professional guidelines.[5]
- **Practice Standards**: RSA 310-A series (NH)—license required, standards of practice, term/renewal, disciplinary action, penalties, limited notarial function.[4] Nevada—prohibits uncertified practice; firms must provide transcripts/recordings to third parties.[6] Kentucky—high personal conduct standards, responsibility to public/courts (Code of Professional Responsibility, adopted July 1999).[8]
- **Remote Reporting**: California pilot projects (13 counties, July 1, 2025–July 1, 2026) require licensed full-time reporters; Judicial Council report due to Legislature.[1][7]

### 4. Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Pain Point: Certification Barriers**—Multi-part exams (e.g., California's 3 parts); Solution: Accept national certs like NCRA RPR (new in CA AB 2783).[1]
- **Pain Point: Training Mandates**—Georgia's LEAP for 2024-2025 licensees risks suspension; Solution: Mandatory Zoom seminar February 23, 2026.[5]
- **Pain Point: Remote/Tech Integration**—Pilot termination July 1, 2026 (CA); grand jury recording bans (KY HB 305, Class A misdemeanor/Class D felony).[1][2] Solution: Nevada SB 191 expands to legal video recorders, requires transcript access.[6]
- **Pain Point: Unauthorized Practice**—Prohibited without certificate (NV NRS 656); Solution: Board enforcement, designation as "certified court reporter."[6]

### 5. Expert Opinions and Real-World Examples
- **California Judicial Council**: Oversees remote pilot results report (due post-July 1, 2026) for 13 superior courts.[1]
- **Georgia Board of Court Reporting**: Enforces ethics/training as "integral to judicial system"; LEAP educates on state rules.[5]
- **Nevada Certified Court Reporters Board**: SB 191 proponents expand scope to video, emphasizing public indication of certified status.[6]
- **Kentucky Court Reporters Association (KYCRA)**: Code stresses law observance/high conduct; real-world: HB 305 bans grand jury recordings (10-year prosecution window).[2][8]

### 7. Recent Trends or Changes (2024-2026)
- **2025 CA AB 2783**: Adds NCRA RPR to satisfy Dictation/Transcription exam; enables remote pilots July 1, 2025–July 1, 2026 (repeal June 1, 2027).[1][7]
- **2025 NV SB 191**: Expands certification to legal video recorders/firms; mandates transcript access; revises "certified court reporter" definition.[6]
- **2026 GA LEAP**: Mandatory for 2024-2025 licensees by Feb 23, 2026.[5]
- **2026 KY HB 305 (proposed Feb 8)**: Grand jury no-record/disclose rule (10-year misdemeanor/felony prosecution).[2]
- Trend: Digital expansion (remote/video) with certification harmonization (national creds); pilot evaluations for admissibility/compliance.[1][6]
