### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- No specific statistics on court reporters testifying as expert witnesses found in results; focus is on general expert witness rules under FRCP 26 and FRE 702.
- Rule 26(a)(2)(B) mandates written reports for retained experts, including list of prior testimony in last **4 years** and publications in last **10 years**[3][10].
- Failure to comply with Rule 26 expert disclosures can lead to exclusion of testimony (e.g., *Goodman v. Praxair, Inc.* (9th Cir. 2011) 653 F.3d 1163, 1176)[10].
- Amendments effective **December 1, 2010**, limit discoverability of draft reports and attorney-expert communications[2][8].

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- **Court reporters as witnesses**: Not directly addressed; standards treat them as hybrid or fact witnesses unless retained as experts. Retained experts must provide Rule 26(a)(2)(B) report with opinions, facts/data considered, exhibits, qualifications, prior cases, and compensation[3][5][10].
- Non-retained experts (e.g., hybrid like treating physicians) require Rule 26(a)(2)(C) disclosure: subject matter and **summary of facts/opinions** (no full report)[4][8].
- **Authentication and deposition video admissibility**: Not covered; implied under FRE 702 for expert opinions and FRCP 26 for disclosures.
- Best practices: Disclose by court deadlines to avoid Rule 37 sanctions; supplement under Rule 26(e)(2) if opinions change[4][5][10].
- Attorneys bear burden to demonstrate expert reliability by **preponderance standard** (more likely than not)[1][7][9].

### 4. Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Pain point**: Ambiguity for "hybrid" witnesses (e.g., court reporters authenticating transcripts)—not "retained" so no full report, but must disclose summary or face exclusion[4].
  - **Solution**: Use Rule 26(a)(2)(C) for subject matter/summary; meet deadlines[4][8].
- **Pain point**: Overbroad prior "data or other information" disclosure; amended to **facts or data only**[2][8].
  - **Solution**: Limit to facts/data considered[2].
- **Pain point**: Draft reports and attorney communications discoverable.
  - **Solution**: 2010 amendments grant work product protection (exceptions: compensation, provided facts/data, assumptions)[2][8].
- **Pain point**: Courts requiring reports from non-retained experts.
  - **Solution**: Clarify via stipulation or court order[8].

### 5. Expert Opinions and Real-World Examples
- **Hybrid witness example**: Treating physicians need only summary disclosures, not full reports, reducing defense burden[4].
- **Committee note (FRE 702)**: Courts must ensure testimony "stays within bounds" of reliable methodology; 2023 amendment emphasizes **preponderance standard** for admissibility[7][9].
- **Advisory Committee**: Rule 702 applies only to expert testimony, not fact witnesses[6].
- No court reporter-specific examples; general: Attorneys must educate on consulted (non-disclosed) vs. testifying experts[3].

### 7. Recent Trends or Changes (2024-2026)
- **2023 FRE 702 amendment** (effective Dec 2023): Proponent must prove by **more likely than not** that testimony is reliable/helpful; emphasizes judicial gatekeeping[1][7][9].
- **2025/2026 FRCP 702 anticipation**: Judges actively evaluate methods; experts limited to analysis scope; attorneys bear burden[1].
- **Jan 29, 2026 Evidence Rules Hearings**: Testimony on Rule 702 scope (expert vs. non-expert)[6].
- Trend: Narrower disclosures, stronger work product protections from 2010 amendments persist; increased scrutiny on reliability post-2023[1][2][9].
