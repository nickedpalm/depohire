### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- U.S. court reporter payroll employment declined from 14,240 in May 2022 to 12,390 in May 2023[1].
- Stenographer workforce shrank 21% over the last decade to 23,000 remaining[4].
- 21,800 court reporters employed in U.S. in 2023, down 2% from 2022; projected to 22,500 by 2032 with 3% annual growth[6].
- Freelance court reporters: 62% of workforce (13,500 individuals) in 2023[6].
- Part-time court reporters: 3,800 (17% of total) in 2023[6].
- Court reporting programs enrolled 4,500 students nationwide in 2023; 1,200 new Registered Professional Reporter (RPR) certifications issued[6].
- Projected shortage: 5,500 certified court reporters by 2030[6].
- BLS projects 17,700 jobs in 2024 with 0% growth to 2034; 1,700 annual openings from replacements[5].
- Global Court Reporting Services market: $1.58B in 2025, $1.66B in 2026, reaching $2.27B by 2032 at 5.35% CAGR[3].
- North America court reporting services: 42% of global revenue ($612M) in 2023[6].
- U.S. freelance court reporter market: $320M in 2023, up 5.8% YoY[6].
- Digital court reporting segment: 12% growth in 2023, 35% of $1.45B total U.S. industry[6].
- Median annual wage: $67,310 ($32.36/hour) in May 2024 (BLS); $63,060 in May 2023[5][6].
- Top 10% earn over $104,030 annually (2023)[6].
- 76% of legal professionals cite scheduling difficulties as top shortage challenge; 55% report increased costs[4].

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- Accuracy as top KPI (96% of respondents)[4].
- Certified digital court reporters use advanced technology, real-time oversight, specialized training, and certification equivalent to stenographers[4].
- AAERT 2025 Report urges regulatory updates to expand digital capture methods for reliable transcripts[4].
- Rising standards include data security/privacy focus, cloud-based systems, AI/machine learning integration[2].
- BLS entry: Postsecondary nondegree award + short-term on-the-job training; no formal experience required[5].

### 5. Expert Opinions and Real-World Examples
- AAERT 2025 Report (April 10, 2025): "Digital court reporters provide accurate, reliable capture... leveraging technology solutions that are continuing to evolve" – advocates regulatory support for digital methods to solve crisis[4].
- Speechmatics (Tom Young, Digital Specialist): Shortage reshaping litigation; legal teams adapting via digital reporting and AI transcription[1].
- California example: Felony cases prioritized; >50% family/probate cases, nearly all major civil cases lack reporters (judicial branch data, 06/30/2025); unions resist digital substitutes[2].
- 76% legal pros surveyed: Scheduling biggest issue; digital as key solution[4].

### 6. State/Regional Variations

- Florida: 1,400 court reporters (6.4% U.S. total, 2023)[6].
- Nationwide U.S. crisis impacts all legal systems; digital acceptance growing variably by state regulations[4].
