## Growth Rates and Projections

**Compound Annual Growth Rates (CAGR):**
- Global court reporting services: 5.35% CAGR through 2032[1]
- U.S. court reporting services: 5.2% CAGR (2025–2032)[8]
- Global court reporting software market: 7.2% CAGR (2022–2030)[2]

The **digital court reporting segment** demonstrated stronger growth, expanding 12% in 2023 and representing 35% of the total U.S. industry[2]. The U.S. freelance court reporter market expanded 5.8% year-over-year in 2023 to USD 320 million[2].

## Employment and Workforce Statistics

**U.S. Court Reporter Employment:**
- 21,800 court reporters employed as of 2023, down 2% from 2022[2]
- Employment projected to grow 3% annually through 2032[2]
- Freelance court reporters comprise 62% of the U.S. workforce, totaling 13,500 individuals in 2023[2]

## End-User Segments

Court reporting services serve a diverse client base[1]:
- Corporate legal departments seeking streamlined transcript access
- Government and judicial bodies focused on record confidentiality
- Independent attorneys requiring cost-effective solutions
- Insurance companies driving volume-based engagements
- Law firms managing large litigation workloads (largest end-user category)[8]
- Regulatory agencies enforcing stringent documentation standards

## Regional Distribution

**Geographic Breakdown:**
- Americas
- Europe, Middle East & Africa
- Asia-Pacific

The Northeast represents the largest U.S. region, while the West is the fastest-growing region[8].

## Market Drivers and Trends

Key growth drivers include[4]:
- Increasing volume of litigation across various sectors
- Rising demand for accurate and unbiased documentation in legal proceedings
- Growing adoption of remote deposition technologies

**Service Demand (2026):**
Court reporting remains the most sought litigation support service at 84% demand, followed by interpreting and translations (55%), record retrieval (48%), transcription (40%), and trial graphics and exhibits (32%)[7].

## Market Characteristics

The U.S. court reporting services market is characterized as **fragmented**, with no dominant players controlling significant market share[8]. This fragmentation reflects the industry's composition of independent practitioners, small firms, and larger service providers competing across different geographic and client segments.
