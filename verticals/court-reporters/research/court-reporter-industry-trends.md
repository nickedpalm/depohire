### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- U.S. stenographer workforce: 23,000 remaining, down 21% over the last decade[2].
- Payroll employment in court reporters/simultaneous captioners: Fell from 14,240 (May 2022) to 12,390 (May 2023)[1].
- Total U.S. court reporters employed: 21,800 as of 2023, down 2% from 2022; projected to grow 3% annually through 2032, reaching 22,500 by 2032[3][7].
- Freelance court reporters: 62% of workforce (13,500 individuals) in 2023[7].
- Projected annual job openings: 1,700 through 2034, mostly from replacements due to retirements/transfers; overall employment growth: 0% (little or no change) 2024-2034[3].
- Projected shortage: 5,500 certified court reporters by 2030[7].
- Market size: USD 1.58 billion in 2025; USD 1.66 billion in 2026; projected to USD 2.27 billion by 2032 at CAGR 5.35%[6].
- Litigation support budgets: 32% of respondents expect increased spending in 2026 (up 3% from prior year)[4].
- Technology adoption (2023): 85% use digital stenography machines; 28% use voice-to-text; 92% use remote deposition platforms[7].
- Certifications (2023): 1,200 new RPR certifications; national pass rate 72%; 4,500 students enrolled in programs[7].
- AI-assisted reporters entering workforce: 800 new in 2023[7].
- Regional: Florida employs 1,400 court reporters (6.4% of U.S. total, 2023)[7].

### 2. Current Pricing/Rates
- Median annual wage for court reporters: $63,060 (May 2023); top 10% earn over $104,030[3][7].
- Hourly median: $32.36[3].
- Freelance average annual: $85,000 (2023)[7].
- Industry impact: 55% of legal professionals report increased costs due to shortage[2].

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- Accuracy as top KPI: 96% of respondents prioritize it[2].
- Certified digital court reporters: Use advanced technology, real-time oversight, specialized training/certification comparable to stenographers[2].
- 85% standard use of digital stenography machines (2023)[7].
- Adoption of cloud-based systems, real-time streaming, AI-powered speech recognition for transcript delivery, data integrity, and e-discovery integration[6].
- National certification pass rate benchmark: 72% (2023)[7].
- Best practices include secure cloud-native platforms for audio-visual records, metadata management, and compliance across jurisdictions[6].

### 5. Expert Opinions and Real-World Examples
- AAERT 2025 Report (industry experts): Calls for expanding digital capture methods to solve nationwide crisis; digital reporters ensure access to justice[2].
- Tom Young (Speechmatics Digital Specialist, Mar 2026): Shortage reshaping litigation; legal teams adapting via digital reporting/AI transcription[1].
- U.S. Legal Support 2026 Survey: 37% expect remote court reporting/depositions to rise; 40% see advanced eDiscovery as top 5-year impact[4].
- California example: Felony cases prioritized; >50% family/probate and most civil cases lack reporters; unions resist digital substitutes (Jun 2025)[5].
- 360iResearch: Providers transformed reporting via AI, cloud, real-time access; differentiation via tech investment/domain expertise[6].
- Gitnux Report: Steady growth via tech demand despite shortages[7].

### 6. State/Regional Variations
- California: Severe shortages; felony priority only; >50% family/probate, most civil cases unreported; unions block substitutes[5].
- Florida: 1,400 reporters (6.4% U.S. total, 2023)[7].
- Nationwide U.S.: Uniform shortage (76% scheduling issues); digital solutions gaining acceptance[2].

### 7. Recent Trends or Changes (2024-2026)
- 2024-2025: Employment decline (21,800 in 2023, down 2%); voice-to-text adoption to 28%; AAERT report highlights digital shift[2][7].
- 2025: Market at USD 1.58B; stenographers at 23,000; 55% cost increases reported[2][6].
- 2026: Market to USD 1.66B; 37% expect remote reporting growth; 32% budget increases; AI/cloud/eDiscovery acceleration; payroll at 12,390 (prior year baseline)[1][4][6].
- Ongoing: Real-time transcription demand; remote services expansion; AI/ML integration; data security focus; arbitration/mediation growth[5].
- Employment outlook: Flat 0% growth to 2034, but 3% annual projection through 2032 via tech[3][7].
