### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- Freelance court reporters with 5+ years experience earn $70,000–$90,000 annually, potentially exceeding $50,000 for entry-level freelancers depending on workload volume[6].
- Official court reporters (salaried courthouse employees) receive steady income with benefits like health insurance, retirement, and paid time off, contrasting freelancers' variable earnings[5][3].
- Freelancers work variable hours (not fixed 40-hour week), often on-call with 1-day notice, leading to feast-or-famine scheduling (e.g., multiple jobs one week, none for weeks)[4][3].

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- **Freelance**: Independent contractors work via agencies for depositions, hearings, meetings; select assignments for skills/availability; market/network for steady work; handle own transcription at home post-assignment[1][5][4].
- **Agency/Firm**: Provides dependable scheduling, stenography/videography; covers broader client needs (law firms, corporations); superior to solo freelancers in transcript handling and reliability[7].
- **Official (for context, not direct hire)**: Full-time court employees with fixed schedules, job security; attorneys hire freelancers/agencies for out-of-court work[1][3].
- Best practices: Freelancers build attorney rapport for repeats; agencies ensure no lost transcripts; both require stenographic skills, but freelancers need business acumen[2][7].
