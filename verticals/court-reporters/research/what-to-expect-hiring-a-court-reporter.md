### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- Nationwide shortage of stenographic court reporters makes same-day emergency bookings increasingly difficult[3].
- Training duration: 2-3 years for stenography/voice writing programs[1]; few months to 4 years total for certification, varying by state and method (digital fastest, traditional longest)[2].
- Federal official court reporter salary (TX, 2026): $95,454–$109,772 (CR Level 1–4)[5].
- NCRA certifications: Registered Professional Reporter (RPR) requires written exam + skills test; Registered Merit Reporter (RMR) and Certified Realtime Reporter (CRR) for higher levels[4][5].

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- **Hiring Process Steps** (from agency perspective)[3]:
  1. Identify needs (e.g., realtime feed, remote, rough draft).
  2. Set timeline—book early due to shortages.
  3. Vet agency for nationwide network, credentialed reporters.
  4. Ask key questions on tech/procedures.
  5. Secure network with high-performing firms for repeat use.
  6. Use single agency for streamlined billing, file transfer, tech support.
- **Reporter Qualifications**:
  | Requirement | Details |
  |-------------|---------|
  | Education | Postsecondary certificate/degree in court reporting (legal terminology, stenography, grammar); 2-year programs common[1][4][6]. |
  | Licensing | State-specific (e.g., CCR in AL, RPR for official in CO); notary public often required[2][3]. |
  | Certifications | NCRA: RPR, RMR, CRR; 4 accrediting associations total[3][5]. |
  | Experience | On-the-job training post-certification, starting with depositions[4]. |
- **Reporting Process** (post-hiring): Prep (setup stenotype/audio, clarify names/terms); realtime verbatim shorthand capture[4].
- Best practice: Work with agencies for vetting, coverage (remote/out-of-state), consistent scheduling[3].

### 4. Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Pain Point: Shortage of stenographers**—harder for same-day jobs[3].  
  **Solution**: Book early via reputable agencies with large networks[3].
- **Pain Point: State-specific requirements**—e.g., stricter in CA/TX (longer cert timelines), AL (mandatory CCR), CO (RPR for official)[2].  
  **Solution**: Check local courts/legal orgs; prioritize licensed/notarized reporters[2][3].
- **Pain Point: Last-minute searches**—ineffective[3].  
  **Solution**: Build relationships with agencies for streamlined repeat hires[3].
- **Pain Point: Tech/procedural mismatches** (realtime, remote)[3].  
  **Solution**: Vet agencies upfront; secure for case-specific needs[3].
