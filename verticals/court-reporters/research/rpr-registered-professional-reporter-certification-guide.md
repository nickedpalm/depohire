# RPR (Registered Professional Reporter) Certification Research Notes

## Definition and Overview
- **RPR** is NCRA's foundational, entry-level certification for freelance/official reporters, students, salary seekers, and license requirements.[1][3][7]
- Establishes competence as a court reporter; only nationally recognized program for reporter skills.[1][5]
- 81% of RPR holders value it personally; 94% see it as important to the profession.[1]
- No minimum education (below Associate's degree) or work experience (≤2 years) required.[4][5]

## Requirements to Obtain
- **Two components**:
  - **Written Knowledge Test (WKT)**: Multiple-choice on reporting technology, procedures, legal terminology, ethics.[3][7]
  - **Online Skills Tests (3 legs, 5 minutes each)**:
    | Test Type       | WPM  | Voices | Accuracy Required |
    |-----------------|------|--------|-------------------|
    | Literary        | 180 | 1     | 95%              |
    | Jury Charge     | 200 | 1     | 95%              |
    | Testimony/Q&A   | 225 | 2     | 95%              |[1][3][7][8]
- Post-dictation: 3 minutes to attach steno notes; 75 minutes to transcribe/submit.[1]
- Supports stenographic or voice writing.[9]
- Must join NCRA as Registered member within 30 days of passing.[1]

## Maintenance and Renewal
- Renew every 3 years via NCRA membership + minimum **3.0 CEUs** (no alternative options like exam).[1][4][5][6]
- Prerequisite for advanced certs: RMR (3 years continuous NCRA membership post-RPR); RDR (6 years post-RMR).[2]

## Pricing and Rates
- No specific exam fees listed; costs tied to NCRA membership/exams (e.g., New Hampshire: "Per NCRA RPR exam and membership fees").[3]
- Nevada state CCR (non-NCRA): $350 application.[3]

## Benefits: Why It Matters
- Entry to advanced NCRA certs (RMR at 240+ WPM; CRR realtime; RDR elite).[2][3][6][9]
- Boosts salary/employment; 8/51 states require it for licensure.[3]
- Majority of certifying states accept RPR (or skills portion) in lieu of state exam.[7]
- Demonstrates 225 WPM minimum capture in literary/jury/testimony.[8]

## When It Doesn't Matter
- Not required in most states: 24/51 mandate state license/certification (NCRA optional); Nebraska voluntary only.[3][7]
- Some states accept RPR skills but require separate state WKT.[7]

## Industry Standards and Best Practices
- NCRA sets national benchmark: 95% accuracy at progressive speeds for entry-level.[1][3]
- RPR prerequisite for CRR (remains mandatory; 180 WPM straight matter at 96%).[2]
- Mock RPR exams recommended pre-testing.[7]
- Core for progression: RPR → RMR (higher WPM) → RDR (experience + tests).[2][3][9]

## Key Statistics and Data Points
- 81% RPR holders: personally valuable.[1]
- 94% RPR holders: important to profession.[1]
- 8/51 states require RPR.[3]
- 24/51 states mandate licensure/certification.[3]
- ~50% U.S. states require certification (NCRA/state exam).[7]
- Renewal: 3.0 CEUs/3 years.[1][6]

## Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Pain: High speeds/accuracy (225 WPM 2-voice at 95%)** → Solution: Mock exams, speed-building practice.[1][7][8]
- **Pain: Transcription time pressure (75 min post-3 min notes)** → Solution: Steno machine proficiency training.[1]
- **Pain: State variations** → Solution: Check state board; use RPR for reciprocity.[3][7]
- **Pain: Renewal CEUs** → Solution: NCRA-approved programs (no exam option).[1][4]
