### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- Median annual wage for court reporters: **$67,310** (May 2024 data).[4]
- National employment: **17,700** court reporters and simultaneous captioners; projected 1,700 annual openings through 2034 due to replacements, not growth (0% change 2024-2034).[4]
- Average initial court reporter licensing fees across 51 jurisdictions: **$158**; range **$25-$500**.[3]
- Cheapest licensing: Illinois (**$25**); most expensive: Arizona (**$500**).[3]
- Federal court reporter starting salaries (effective Jan 12, 2026, for hires before Oct 11, 2009): Level 1 ranges **$81,543-$107,930** across localities; Level 5 up to **$129,517**.[1]
- Florida state-funded rates (FY 2024-25): Deposition appearance 1st hour **$75**, 2nd+ hours **$50**; regular transcript **$4.00/page**.[6]

### 2. Current Pricing/Rates (2026)
- Certified court reporter standard transcripts: **$4.50-$7.00/page**; appearance fees **$150-$400**; hourly **$75-$150**; rough drafts **$2.00-$3.50/page**; expedited +50-100%.[2]
- New York: Appearance **$250-$400**, per-page **$6.50-$7.50** (higher in NYC vs. upstate).[2]
- Washington DC: Per-page **$6.00-$7.50**, appearance **$350**.[2]
- Maine: Per-page **$5.00-$6.50** (travel fees in rural areas).[2]
- Delaware: Variable based on stenographer availability.[2]
- Florida state-funded (post-July 1, 2024): Video services **$150/hour** per location (2-hour min.); deposition transcript regular **$4.00/page**.[6]
- US District Court NJ maximums (effective Oct 1, 2023): Original transcript rates unspecified in detail, but appellate transcripts reduced 10% if filed >45 days late, 20% if >60 days.[8]
- Licensing fees examples: California **$265**, Nevada **$350**, Michigan **$285**, Louisiana **$265**, Georgia **$125**.[3]

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- Federal Judicial Conference sets maximum per-page transcription rates for official court reporters (e.g., District of NJ guidelines).[8]
- Billing tied to court order dates for transcripts; appearance fees based on service date (Florida JAC model).[6]
- Certifications required in varying degrees; 25 states have fixed government fees **$25-$500**; 26 states variable or N/A.[3]
- Stenographic reporting remains standard for word-for-word accuracy in trials/depositions, with digital/AI as supplements.[7]

### 4. Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Shortage crisis**: 2023-24 issued only **121 new licenses** in one state; programs dropped from 17 to **8**.[7]
  - Solutions: Digital reporting, AI transcription for legal teams.[7]
- High demand in urban areas (NYC, DC) drives premiums; rural scarcity (Maine) adds travel fees.[2]
- Delayed transcripts penalized: 10% reduction if >45 days, 20% if >60 days post-settlement.[8]
- Cost variability by complexity, location, delivery speed.[2]

### 5. Expert Opinions and Real-World Examples
- CourtScribes (2026 guide): NY/DC rates highest due to "strong legal market demand" and federal complexity; Maine lower but availability-limited.[2]
- BLS: Little growth due to tech alternatives, but steady replacements needed.[4]
- Florida JAC (HB 5001, July 2024): Increased video/deposition rates to **$150/hour** and **$50/hour** add-ons for sustainability.[6]
- Speechmatics analysis: Shortage "reshaping litigation"; AI/digital as adaptation.[7]
