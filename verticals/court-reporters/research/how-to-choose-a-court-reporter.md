### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- **NCRA Certifications**: RPR (entry-level) requires skills test at 180 WPM literary (95% accuracy), 200 WPM jury charge, 225 WPM testimony (two-voice).[2][3][5]
- **State Licensing**: 24 of 51 states mandate state license or certification; 8 of 51 require NCRA RPR.[2]
- **Speed Requirements**: Minimum stenography speeds: 225 WPM literary, 200 WPM jury charge, 180 WPM testimony in most programs; Nevada CCR: 200-225 WPM four-voice Q&A at 97.5% accuracy.[2][5]
- **Experience Thresholds**: Federal courts require 1 year prime court reporting experience (e.g., depositions, grand jury); California eligibility: 12 months full-time (1,400 hours documented) in multiple-voice proceedings.[1][6]
- **Federal Salary Levels (2026)**: CR Level 1: $95,454 (RPR); Level 2: $100,227 (merit cert); Level 3: $104,999 (realtime cert); Level 4: $109,772 (realtime + merit).[1]
- **Continuing Education**: RSR holders: 3.0 CEUs every 3 years; Nevada: 15 hrs; New Hampshire: 30 hrs.[2][3]

### 2. Current Pricing/Rates
- **Federal Official Reporters**: Base salary $95,454–$114,546 annually (plus transcript fees set by Judicial Conference); levels tied to certifications (e.g., CR3: $104,999 with CRR).[1]
- **Nevada Licensing Fee**: $350 for CCR exam.[2]
- **No freelance/deposition rates in results**; federal reporters provide own equipment/supplies, charge transcript fees separately.[1]

### 4. Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Pain Point: Variable State Requirements** – 24 states license, others voluntary; inconsistent speeds/exams. *Solution*: Prioritize NCRA RPR (accepted by many states/federal).[2][3][5]
- **Pain Point: Experience Verification** – California requires 1,400 documented hours in multiple-voice work. *Solution*: Submit employer letters (dates, duties, % time) + job sheets.[6]
- **Pain Point: Equipment/Self-Supply** – Federal reporters must provide all gear. *Solution*: Select reporters with proven setup reliability.[1]
- **Pain Point: Transcription Time/Accuracy** – Nevada: 3 hrs for 10-min test at 97.5%. *Solution*: Verify realtime certs (CRR) for faster delivery.[1][2]
- **Pain Point: Probation/Team Fit** – 1-year federal probation; irregular hours. *Solution*: Check references for availability/teamwork.[1]

### 5. Expert Opinions and Real-World Examples
- **NCRA Stance**: "Judicial system depends on qualified court reporters"; upgraded CP standards in 1973 led to federal 5% salary bump for 5-year CM holders.[3]
- **Federal Courts (LAED Example)**: Prefers CRR + merit; requires RPR minimum + 1 year experience; salary incentives for advanced certs.[1]
- **California Board**: Accepts RPR for dictation portion (per AB2783); NVRA CVR skips skills exam (AB3252, eff. Jan 2025).[4][6][10]
- **Program Standards**: 2-4 year associate/certificate; 97% accuracy focus.[2][5]
- **USCRA**: Offers 4x/year FCRR realtime tests for federal salary increases.[9]

### 7. Recent Trends or Changes (2024-2026)
- **AB2783 (CA, 2025-2026)**: Adds NCRA RPR to satisfy dictation/transcription exam (previously only NVRA CVR/CVR-S).[4][10]
- **AB3252 (CA, eff. Jan 1, 2025)**: NVRA CVR/CVR-S holders skip dictation/skills exam (written only).[6]
- **Education Shift**: 2-4 year programs emphasize realtime (CRR) over legacy CP (upgraded 1973).[2][3]
- **Federal Incentives**: Realtime (CRR/FCRR) boosts salary to $104,999+; USCRA tests 4x/year.[1][9]
- **NCRA Expansion**: Assists states with standards; RPR as de facto national entry since 1937, increasingly state-accepted.[3]
