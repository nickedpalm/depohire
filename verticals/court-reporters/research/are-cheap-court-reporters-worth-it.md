### 1. Key Statistics and Data Points
- US has approximately **17,700 court reporters** total, with retirements outpacing new entrants, creating a structural shortage[4].
- California courts: **1,101 court-employed reporters** as of 2026, needing **458 additional full-time reporters** to meet caseload demands[7].
- California hires (Jan 2023–Jun 2025): **286.2 FTE court reporters**, of which **112.4 FTE (39.3%)** were voice writers[7].
- California court reporter median total salary + benefits: **$205,030.91**; private hire costs: **$2,580/day (deposition)**, **$3,300/day (trial)**[7].
- California courts spent **$25.2 million** on transcripts in FY 2024–25[7].
- Court reporter licensing fees (51 jurisdictions): range **$25–$500**, average **$158**; cheapest: Illinois (**$25**), most expensive: Arizona (**$500**)[1].
- Standard deposition transcript length: **50–150 pages**[2].

### 3. Industry Standards and Best Practices
- **Certifications**: National (RPR, RMR) often required; state-specific licensing mandatory in 25+ states[1].
- **Pricing Structure**: Appearance fees + per-page transcripts; minimum 2–4 hours for live reporting; bundled services for savings[2].
- **Quality Assurance**: Use program-trained reporters with specialized software for searchable realtime transcripts; avoid transcription from recordings due to lower accuracy on unclear audio[2].
- **Hiring Criteria**: Prioritize experience, certifications, subject-matter expertise, technology (e.g., realtime feeds)[2].
- **Recruitment Incentives**: Signing bonuses up to **$50,000**, loan/equipment allowances (**$15,000**), finder's fees (**$25,000**), tuition scholarships[7].

### 4. Common Pain Points and Solutions
- **Pain Point: Shortage**: Retirements > new entrants; California felony cases prioritized, leaving >50% family/probate and most civil cases without reporters[4][6][7].
- **Solution**: Digital reporting, AI transcription; remote services; voice writers (39.3% of recent CA hires)[4][6][7].
- **Pain Point: High Costs for Quality**: Private hires 51% above court salaries; competition drives up rates[7].
- **Solution**: Bundled services, program-trained reporters; in-house training programs with guaranteed employment[2][7].
- **Pain Point: Inaccuracy/Reliability**: Cheap audio transcription fails on unclear audio; no live responsiveness[2].
- **Solution**: Certified stenographers with realtime tech over budget audio options[2].
- **Pain Point: Transcript Access**: Courts spend millions; 30% fee increase in 2021[7].
- **Solution**: Prioritize official reporters for critical cases[7].
