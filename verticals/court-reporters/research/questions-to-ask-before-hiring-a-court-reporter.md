# Research Notes: Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Court Reporter

## Key Questions and Evaluation Criteria

### Reporter Qualifications and Credentials

**Essential certifications to verify:**[1]
- Registered Professional Reporter (RPR)
- Certified Realtime Reporter (CRR)
- Certified Realtime Captioner (CRC)
- State Certified Shorthand Reporter (CSR) license

Request recent cases with similar characteristics, including case type (civil trials, complex depositions), venue, duration, topic, and transcript delivery method (real-time or daily).[1]

Review a sample package from the court reporting firm containing title page, condensed transcript with word index, and exhibit index to assess output quality.[1]

### Professional Conduct and Accuracy Standards

**Critical professionalism indicators:**[2]
- Punctuality and prompt transcript turnaround
- Assertiveness in controlling the deposition environment
- Willingness to request clarification when audio is unclear
- Proactive spelling verification of unfamiliar terms
- Confirmation of on/off-record status without hesitation

Court reporters function as officers of the court with a duty to ensure record accuracy, which directly impacts settlement negotiations and witness examination outcomes.[2]

### Technical Expertise Requirements

**For complex litigation cases:**[2]
Court reporters should demonstrate familiarity with highly technical terminology relevant to the case type. Cases involving mass torts, pharmaceuticals, medical malpractice, and intellectual property require specialized knowledge to ensure complete and accurate records for trial or appeal purposes.[2]

**Core technical competencies to assess:**[3]
- Stenography proficiency and stenotype machine operation
- Transcription software expertise (specific tools like CaseCAT or Eclipse)
- Audio processing capabilities for difficult audio conditions and overlapping speech
- Real-time reporting capabilities
- Proofreading and error-catching methodology

### Operational and Procedural Fit

**Remote and hybrid deposition considerations:**[1]
- Location of the deposition officer
- Oath administration procedures
- Recording oversight responsibility
- Sealed item storage methods
- Overflow arrangements for multi-day proceedings
- Contingency plans for sessions extending beyond scheduled time

**Workflow management questions:**[1]
- Read-back procedures
- Acceptance of terminology lists for dictionary preparation
- Single point of contact designation for technical issues (access, audio problems)
- Conflict policies and booking confirmation procedures

### Transcript Delivery and Data Security

**Deliverable specifications to confirm:**[1]
- Transcript format requirements
- Delivery windows and turnaround times
- Data security protocols for sealed exhibits
- Errata handling procedures
- Filing hygiene standards

## Industry Standards and Best Practices

**Interview preparation framework for court reporters:**[3]
Candidates should demonstrate proficiency in:
- Legal terminology and courtroom jargon
- Courtroom procedures (witness swearing-in, exhibit marking, sensitive information handling)
- Time management during fast-paced proceedings
- Strict confidentiality and record security maintenance
- Grammar and punctuation standards
- Technology proficiency across multiple platforms

**Behavioral assessment approach:**[4]
Structured interviews typically employ job-related behavioral and situational-based questions asked consistently across all candidates, including:
- Resume and job experience review
- Past decision-making scenarios
- Disagreement resolution with supervisors
- Service quality complaint handling
- Competing deadline management

## Accuracy and Quality Assurance

**Transcription accuracy evaluation:**[3]
Court reporters should outline their specific process including:
- Tools and software used for transcription
- Terminology and name verification methods
- Handling procedures for difficult audio or multiple speakers
- Proofreading and editing approach
- Compliance verification with legal standards

**Pressure performance capability:**[5]
Assess ability to maintain accuracy in high-stress environments using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to evaluate:
- Specific pressure situations faced
- Steps taken to ensure accuracy despite pressure
- Focus and organization techniques employed
- Outcomes and lessons learned

## Conflict and Reference Verification

**Pre-engagement confirmation:**[1]
- Brief reference list review
- Relevant conflict policies
- Inclusion of all details in booking confirmation to establish clear expectations before notice distribution
