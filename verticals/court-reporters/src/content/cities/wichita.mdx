## How to Choose a Court Reporter in Wichita

**Check their certifications first.** RPR (Registered Professional Reporter) and RMR (Registered Merit Reporter) are the baseline—they've passed rigorous exams and committed to continuing education. If you need realtime transcription (text appearing on screens as testimony happens), look specifically for CRR (Certified Realtime Reporter) credentials. This isn't optional theater; it's the difference between attorneys catching an important contradiction live or missing it entirely.

**Ask about their equipment and backup plan.** A professional reporter should carry redundant recording systems—stenotype machine plus digital backup, or multiple recording devices. If their single laptop dies, so does your deposition. Ask directly: "What happens if your primary equipment fails?" If they hesitate, move on.

**Confirm turnaround time in writing.** "Rush" and "expedited" mean different things to different people. A rough draft in 3 days? Five business days for a clean transcript? Get it in the email confirmation. Court Reporters in Wichita's legal market are competitive enough that you can push for 48-hour turnaround on straightforward depositions—just expect to pay more for it.

**Verify they know your subject matter.** A reporter unfamiliar with oil and gas terminology (relevant to Kansas business) will spend hours cleaning up a transcript. Ask about their experience with your industry. The best ones keep specialized glossaries.

**Pro Tip:** Call directly instead of using booking platforms for anything urgent. Wichita's court reporter pool is tight enough that a three-minute conversation tells you more than an online profile. Ask how many depositions they handle per month and whether they're booked solid. Someone running at 80% capacity is more reliable than someone claiming to take everything.

## What to Expect

Court Reporters in Wichita charge between $250–$500+ per session depending on whether it's straightforward or requires realtime reporting, expedited delivery, or specialized expertise. A deposition might run 3–4 hours; trials can stretch days. Transcript costs come on top—typically 10–15 cents per page for standard delivery, more for expedited. 

Realtime reporting (if you need it) runs an additional $100–$200 per session but lets everyone in the room see testimony as it's spoken. Rough drafts arrive within 24–48 hours; final, certified transcripts take 5–10 business days unless you've paid for rush service.

> **Reality Check:** Some reporters quote low session fees, then bury the real cost in transcript page rates and rush fees. Get a full quote upfront. Ask: "What's the all-in price for a typical four-hour deposition with a certified transcript in five business days?" The answer tells you everything.
