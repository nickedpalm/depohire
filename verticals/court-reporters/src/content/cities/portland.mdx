## How to Choose a Court Reporter in Portland

**Look for active certifications.** RPR (Registered Professional Reporter) is the baseline — it means they've passed the National Court Reporters Association exam and maintain continuing education. RMR, RDR, and CRR certifications signal deeper expertise in merit reporting, realtime, and specialized formats. In Oregon, CSR (Certified Shorthand Reporter) is also relevant depending on the context. Ask which certifications they hold and when they were last renewed. Expired credentials aren't credentials.

**Ask about their equipment and backup plan.** Modern court reporters use stenotype machines, voice writing systems, or digital recording — each has tradeoffs for accuracy and realtime capability. The real question: what happens if their primary equipment fails during your deposition? A professional will have a backup. If they don't, move on.

**Verify they know Portland courts and procedures.** Local knowledge matters. They should understand Multnomah County rules, federal court (District of Oregon) protocols, and the specific judges or arbitrators you're working with. If they've covered similar cases in your practice area, even better. Ask for references from other Portland-based attorneys.

**Check turnaround times and pricing structure.** Rough draft vs. final transcript, realtime vs. daily copy, expedited delivery — these all affect cost and timeline. Rates typically range from $250 to $1,500+ per session depending on complexity and services. Get the quote in writing. Hidden charges for expediting or realtime feeds are a sign of a shop that's trying to nickel-and-dime you.

**Pro Tip:** Call 2-3 reporters before you book. You're not just evaluating their credentials — you're testing responsiveness and professionalism. If someone is slow to return your call or vague about pricing, they'll likely be the same way during your case.

## What to Expect

A typical court reporting session runs $250–$500 for a standard deposition (2–4 hours), climbing to $1,500+ if you need realtime reporting, expedited delivery, or coverage of complex multi-day trials. You'll provide the reporter with case caption, location, parties, and expected duration; they'll show up 15 minutes early with their rig. After the session, turnaround depends on what you ordered — rough draft same day, final transcript within 5–7 business days, or expedited (24–48 hours) at premium rates.

**Reality Check:** Don't assume the cheapest quote is the best deal. A reporter charging $200/session might be newer, less reliable, or cutting corners on transcript quality. The cost of fixing a botched deposition transcript — or worse, discovering errors during trial — far exceeds paying a bit more upfront for someone with proven track record.
