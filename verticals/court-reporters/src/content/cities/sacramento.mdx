## How to Choose a Court Reporter in Sacramento

**Check certifications first.** RPR (Registered Professional Reporter) is the gold standard in California — it means someone passed the National Court Reporters Association exam and meets ongoing education requirements. RMR (Registered Merit Reporter) and RDR (Registered Diplomate Reporter) are higher tiers. Don't skip this step. An uncertified reporter might be cheaper, but you're buying risk, not a deal.

**Ask about specialization.** Some reporters dominate depositions. Others specialize in real-time reporting (meaning the attorneys see testimony stream in on screens as it happens). A few handle video depositions with RPR/RMR/CRR certification. Know what you need before you call — it narrows the pool fast.

**Turnaround matters more than you think.** Ask upfront: rough draft in 5 days or 10? Expedited transcript available? Some reporters build rush fees into their pricing; others don't. Sacramento's court docket moves fast, and a three-week turnaround can kill your case timeline.

**Get pricing in writing.** Session rates run $250–$1,500+ depending on complexity, length, and whether you're paying for expedited delivery or real-time reporting. Ask about minimum charges (many require a half-day or full-day minimum even for short depositions), cancellation policies, and whether rough drafts are included or extra.

**Pro Tip:** Check if they're affiliated with a court reporting agency or independent. Agencies handle scheduling and billing but take a cut. Independents are sometimes cheaper but require more direct management on your end.

## What to Expect

A typical deposition runs 3–8 hours. The reporter shows up with their stenotype machine (or voice writing setup), swears in the witness, and captures everything on the record. You get a rough draft within days, a final transcript in 1–3 weeks depending on complexity and page count. Real-time reporting costs more but delivers live text — worth it for high-stakes proceedings or when you need to react to testimony on the fly.

**Reality Check:** "Cheapest" and "fastest" rarely live in the same person. If someone's quoting half-price and promising same-day turnaround on a 100-page transcript, something's off. Budget accordingly and book early if you have a specific deadline.
