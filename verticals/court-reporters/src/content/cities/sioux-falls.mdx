## How to Choose a Court Reporter in Sioux Falls

**Check certifications first.** RPR (Registered Professional Reporter) and CSR (Certified Shorthand Reporter) are the gold standard. RMR, RDR, and CRR matter if you need specific capabilities — realtime reporting, for instance, requires CRR certification and can run $400–600+ per session but gets you live, verbatim text as testimony happens. If you're doing video depositions, ask about RPR/RMR/CRR (Certified court reporting Specialist) qualification. This isn't gatekeeping — it's the difference between someone who passed a competency exam and someone who didn't.

**Confirm they handle your proceeding type.** A reporter who specializes in civil depositions may not be your best fit for a criminal trial. Ask directly: How many depositions/trials/arbitrations have they done? What's their turnaround on rough drafts? Some excel at expedited transcripts (48–72 hours); others work slower. Know what you need before you call.

**Nail down availability and backup.** Sioux Falls's legal market is concentrated but active — the bigger firms book the reliable reporters weeks out. Ask if they have a preferred backup or network of other reporters they trust. A pro won't leave you hanging if something goes sideways.

**Pro Tip:** Ask about their equipment and backup system. Digital recording failure is rare but catastrophic. Reporters using dual-system capture (stenotype + digital backup, or voice writing + digital) are less likely to produce a garbled transcript. It's a small detail that separates thorough professionals from folks just getting by.

## What to Expect

Pricing runs $250–500 for a standard deposition, $800–1,500+ for trial work, with expedited transcripts, realtime reporting, and video services adding 30–50% to the base rate. Most reporters charge per page on the final transcript, per hour on-site, or a flat session fee — clarify which before booking. Turnaround on a rough draft is typically 3–5 business days; certified finals take longer.

**Reality Check:** Cheap isn't a deal; it's a warning sign. A $200 flat rate for a half-day deposition is someone underselling the work, and undersold work attracts cutting corners. You're paying for accuracy, speed, and someone who shows up prepared. Budget accordingly.
