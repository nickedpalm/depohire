## How to Choose a Court Reporter in San Diego

**Check for California certifications.** The gold standard is CSR (Certified Shorthand Reporter) — California's official credential. RPR (Registered Professional Reporter), RMR (Registered Merit Reporter), and RDR (Registered Diplomate Reporter) are also strong signals of ongoing education and professional standards. Ask for proof. If someone deflects, move on.

**Define your format upfront.** Realtime reporting (text appearing on screen as testimony happens) costs more but saves review time — useful for complex depositions or when you need rough drafts same-day. Digital recording or traditional stenotype transcript work is standard for most proceedings. Know which you need before you call.

**Verify local court experience.** San Diego County courts, federal court (Southern District), and the specific courtroom or deposition venue matter. A reporter familiar with local judges, procedures, and building logistics moves faster and catches procedural details others miss. Ask about their last three jobs in your jurisdiction.

**Get pricing in writing.** Rates typically run $250–$500 for routine depositions, $500–$1,500+ for complex or expedited work. Travel, rush delivery, and realtime add cost. Don't accept verbal estimates. A professional outfit quotes in writing with a breakdown of fees (per page, per hour, setup, rush premium, etc.) before you're on the hook.

**Pro Tip:** Ask about rough draft turnaround. "Same business day" is the bar. If they can't commit, that's information about their workflow and capacity.

## What to Expect

Court Reporters in San Diego typically charge per session (deposition or hearing) or per page of transcript, with additional fees for realtime reporting, expedited delivery, or same-day rough drafts. A routine civil deposition runs 2–4 hours and costs $400–$800; expedited transcripts or realtime reporting can push that to $1,200 or more. Most reputable firms deliver rough drafts within 24 hours and certified transcripts within 5–7 business days unless you pay a premium for faster turnaround.

**Reality Check:** If someone quotes you less than $250 per session without caveats, they're either new, undercharging to build a book, or cutting corners on transcript quality. It's not always a red flag — sometimes it's a small operator with low overhead — but verify their turnaround and accuracy track record before you commit.
