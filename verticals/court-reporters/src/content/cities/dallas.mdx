## How to Choose a Court Reporter in Dallas

**Check certifications first.** RPR (Registered Professional Reporter) is the baseline — it means they've passed rigorous exams and commit to continuing education. RMR (Registered Merit Reporter) and RDR (Registered Diplomate Reporter) indicate even deeper expertise. If you need realtime reporting (text appearing on screens as testimony happens), look for CRR (Certified Realtime Reporter). For video depositions, RPR/RMR/CRR (Certified court reporting Specialist) matters. CSR (Certified Shorthand Reporter) is Texas-specific and widely respected. Don't hire someone without at least one of these.

**Confirm turnaround expectations upfront.** Standard rough drafts typically arrive within 48-72 hours; expedited service costs more but exists for a reason. If you need realtime reporting, that's a premium service — the reporter is managing stenotype machine *and* real-time transmission simultaneously. Get this in writing before you book.

**Ask about their setup.** Do they use stenotype machines, voice writing, or digital recording? Do they handle video depositions in-house or subcontract? For high-stakes cases, you want someone with redundant equipment — backups matter when testimony is irretrievable.

**Verify they know Texas rules.** Texas court reporters operate under specific state procedures, evidence codes, and ethical guidelines. Someone licensed in-state will navigate these without fumbling. Ask them directly about their experience with depositions *and* trial work — they're different skill sets.

> **Pro Tip:** Call ahead and ask which law firms or major corporations they've worked for. Established reporters will have clients they can reference. If they're evasive or vague, that's a flag.

## What to Expect

Sessions run $250–$1,500+ depending on complexity, location (in-office vs. remote), and urgency. A routine deposition in a Dallas law office will land on the lower end; multi-day trials, expedited transcripts, and realtime reporting push toward the higher end. Rough drafts are cheaper than certified, final transcripts; plan accordingly. Most professionals require a deposit to hold the date.

> **Reality Check:** The cheapest quote isn't the best deal. A reporter who underprices may cut corners on turnaround or accuracy. Mid-range pricing ($400–$700 per session for standard work) usually signals someone who's established, reliable, and not desperate for work.
